01

Executive Summary and Group Overview

ShinyHunters is a financially motivated data-theft and extortion brand active since April 2020. It began as a French-origin crew selling stolen databases in bulk (Tokopedia, Wattpad, Microsoft private GitHub repositories), became the administrator of the BreachForums marketplace in 2023 to 2025, and since mid-2025 has operated as the extortion and publication front for identity-first intrusions against SaaS and enterprise platforms: Salesforce, Snowflake-adjacent tenants, Microsoft 365, Instructure Canvas and Oracle PeopleSoft.

The group does not encrypt. It steals data from cloud applications, usually after voice phishing (vishing) a help desk or employee, and demands payment in Bitcoin within 72 hours under threat of publication on a Tor leak site. Four waves of law enforcement action (a 2021 US indictment, French arrests in 2022 and June 2025, and a joint FBI and French seizure of its Salesforce leak site in October 2025) removed identifiable operators and forum infrastructure without stopping the brand. It posted new victims through 22 September 2026.

Analyst Assessment. ShinyHunters is best treated as a brand, not a fixed crew. The name has covered at least three separately evidenced cohorts: the 2020 to 2021 conspiracy charged in Seattle, the BreachForums administrators arrested in France in June 2025, and the 2025 to 2026 cloud intrusion operators that Google tracks as UNC6240. Continuity across cohorts is demonstrated for the brand, the extortion model and parts of the infrastructure; it is not demonstrated for the people. The structural leverage points are the ones that survived every disruption: Russian bulletproof hosting for the leak site, a small number of access-provider handoffs, and Bitcoin cash-out through bridges and instant exchanges.
Naming discipline. Three traps recur in secondary reporting. (1) CrowdStrike's SHINING SPIDER is the Lorenz ransomware operator, not ShinyHunters. (2) Google's UNC6671 (the BlackFile, Falcon and Helix brands) uses ShinyHunters tradecraft but is assessed as operating independently. (3) The ShinyHunters name on a ransom letter is not attribution: the PowerSchool attacker used it without any court-established link to the group.
CONFIRMED. ShinyHunters is an active, financially motivated extortion brand. Google, Microsoft, Unit 42, Europol and MITRE ATT&CK (group G1057, created 29 July 2026) all document it independently.

03

Operational Model

ShinyHunters has used four monetization channels, in roughly this order over time. Each channel is documented; their relative revenue is not.

  • Bulk data resale (2020 to 2021). Plea records show one victim's data was sold 13 times at $5,000. Sales ran through RaidForums, Empire Market and Exploit.
  • Direct extortion. The largest ransom in the 2020 to 2021 case was $425,000. Current demands run from hundreds of thousands to $55.2 million, with a 72-hour Bitcoin deadline.
  • Forum administration. Control of BreachForums gave the brand reach, reputation and a buyer base.
  • Extortion as a service. Unit 42 reports that Bling Libra runs extortion for partner intrusion crews for a 25 to 30 percent revenue share.
Division of Labor

Current campaigns separate access, intrusion and extortion. Microsoft's Storm-3121 performs initial access that leads to both ShinyHunters and Falcon extortion. Google tracks the UNC6040 and UNC6661 intrusion clusters separately from the UNC6240 extortion stage. The extortion front is the part that carries the ShinyHunters name.

ANALYST INFERENCE. Because access providers such as Storm-3121 serve more than one extortion brand, disrupting the ShinyHunters leak site alone does not remove the intrusion supply. Access providers are the higher-yield target.
Negotiation and Pressure
  • Demands arrive by email from ShinyHunters-branded addresses, with Tox as the negotiation channel and samples on file-sharing services.
  • Pressure escalates through public leak-site listings, harassment and defacement. The Canvas ransom note was injected into a custom theme and was visible to roughly 300 organizations.
  • Instructure (Canvas) confirmed it reached an agreement and had the data returned on 11 May 2026. The amount was not disclosed.
  • Salesforce publicly refused to pay for the October 2025 leak-site campaign.
Attribution caution: impersonation. The brand is used by others. The PowerSchool attacker (Matthew Lane, sentenced October 2025) signed ransom letters as ShinyHunters; no court record connects him to the group. ShinyHunters itself has publicly disowned activity under its name, including after its shinyhunte.rs domain was suspended in May 2026. A ransom letter, email signature or leak-site post is a brand claim, not attribution.

Case Study: Instructure Canvas, April to May 2026

Instructure reported that the first intrusion began with malicious code submitted through a support ticket from a Free-For-Teacher account. When staff opened it, a cross-site scripting flaw yielded an authorization token and elevated access (access window 25 to 30 April, detected 29 April). On 7 May the actor re-entered through a second cross-site scripting path in the discussion feature and OAuth flow; it was contained in about ten minutes. Data taken included usernames, email addresses, course names, enrollment data and messages, but not course content, submissions or credentials. Instructure said the incidents "appear to have been perpetrated by" ShinyHunters.

Encryption: ShinySp1d3r

In November 2025 Unit 42 reported ShinySp1d3r, a ransomware-as-a-service product under development by SLSH, with a working Windows build and Linux and ESXi versions pending. No confirmed deployment against a victim has been reported through September 2026, and no decryptor exists or is needed.

CREDIBLE. ShinyHunters has the intent to add encryption. The capability is unproven in the field.
CIS Exclusion

No Russian-language exclusion logic, keyboard-layout check or stated ban on post-Soviet victims has been reported. This group does not follow the Russian-speaking ransomware norm, which is consistent with its French and English-speaking origins.

The AT&T Flow and Its Attribution Problem

Receipt. On 17 May 2024 AT&T paid about 5.72 BTC for deletion of call records stolen from its Snowflake tenant, negotiated through a researcher known as "Reddington". TRM Labs confirmed the transaction.

Layering. TRM traced about $150,000 to two centralized exchanges, under $10,000 to a non-custodial exchange, a small amount to a gambling service, and most of the rest through swap services. TRM said there was "no indication of who controlled the wallets." The Record's headline mentions mixers, but the article does not say mixers were used in this flow.

Extraction. No public source names the exchanges, account holders or final off-ramps.

Attribution caution. The AT&T payment is usually cited as ShinyHunters' best on-chain case. The ShinyHunters label comes from a single media source (Wired, repeated by TRM). The Justice Department charged the Snowflake extortion, including AT&T, to Connor Moucka ("Judische", "Waifu") and John Binns; Moucka pleaded guilty in August 2026 and is due for sentencing on 27 October 2026. Neither the charging documents nor Mandiant's UNC5537 report use the ShinyHunters name. This profile treats the payment as CONFIRMED and its attribution to ShinyHunters as CREDIBLE.

Russian Intelligence Services Nexus

None identified. No government, court or vendor source ties ShinyHunters to tasking, protection or direction by any state. Its targeting, which includes French victims and a claimed intrusion into the FBI, is inconsistent with the Russian safe-harbor model.

The Russia point that does exist is infrastructure, not people. The leak site's clear-web mirrors run on PROSPERO and Proton66, two related St Petersburg bulletproof networks. PROSPERO also appears in the upstream chains of bulletproof hosts in this library's BPH layer. A Western, non-Russian crew renting Russian bulletproof hosting shows those providers serve paying customers regardless of origin.
