01

Executive Summary and Group Overview

INC Ransom, Lynx and Sinobi are three brands built on one codebase and run by overlapping Russian-speaking personnel. As of 18 October 2026 only INC is operating at scale: 980 leak-site listings, victims in 73 countries, and daily posts through 15 October 2026. Lynx has not posted since 29 August 2026 (417 listings lifetime). Sinobi has been dark since 8 May 2026 with every tracked Sinobi site offline (274 listings lifetime). No vendor has announced a shutdown of either Lynx or Sinobi; the dormancy is observed from trackers, not declared. The pattern is consistent with consolidation of activity back onto the INC brand, but no source states that directly. [31] Confirmed: tracker data Inference: consolidation

INC is now a top-five global ransomware brand by listing volume (roughly 29 to 46 new listings a month across 2026), with a documented tilt toward healthcare, professional services, local government and Five Eyes targets. Its 2026 intake runs through two documented supply lines, neither of them INC's own intrusion work: the FortiBleed Fortinet credential-theft operation, whose initial-access brokers feed INC/Lynx and Payload (FBI and US Secret Service, 6 October 2026), and a SonicWall SMA 1000 zero-day exploitation cluster (CVE-2026-15409 and CVE-2026-15410, July 2026). [2][13][15][16][32]

Government output on this lineage is thin but real. A joint Australian ACSC, New Zealand NCSC and CERT Tonga advisory (6 March 2026) describes the INC franchise model, documents the June 2025 Tonga Ministry of Health attack, and names a Russia-based individual as the controller of the data-exfiltration infrastructure used in that incident. The FBI and US Secret Service advisory of 6 October 2026 writes the two brands as a single entity, "INC/Lynx". There are no arrests, indictments, sanctions, rewards or infrastructure seizures naming any of the three brands. [1][2]

What changed since the May 2026 profile: the brand picture inverted (INC active, Lynx and Sinobi dormant); Sinobi is added as the third brand; two government advisories now name the lineage; two initial-access supply lines are documented; the RAMP recruitment venue was seized (28 January 2026); INC's encryptors were rewritten in Rust with multi-architecture Linux builds; and Microsoft tied a Lynx/INC affiliate to the new DeadLock encryptor. Section 11 adds a structural leverage assessment.

02

Lineage and Organizational Heritage

Core Assessment
Confirmed: one technical lineage
Credible: one core team across all three brands

INC Ransom emerged in mid 2023 as a double-extortion operation. Lynx appeared in mid 2024 with heavy INC code reuse and built out a structured RaaS program. Sinobi appeared around June 2025 and is assessed by eSentire, Blackpoint, Barracuda and SOCRadar as a Lynx rebrand or offshoot on the same code. Confidence that the three are one technical lineage is CONFIRMED on code comparison alone. Confidence that the same core team runs all of them is CREDIBLE, resting on a shared registration email, a single operator observed in both the INC and Lynx negotiation panels, and the FBI's decision to write "INC/Lynx" as one entity. Rapid7 is the notable holdout, treating the INC-to-Lynx overlap as consistent with a source-code purchase rather than proof of the same operators. [6][7][8][9][10][13][19][21]

Evidentiary Pillars
Pillar 1: Confirmed
Code Overlap, INC to Lynx
Windows: 48% of functions overall and 70.8% of shared functions (Unit 42 BinDiff, corroborated by Fortinet, Nextron and Blackpoint). Linux and ESXi: 87% overall and 91% on the Linux encryptor (Group-IB, Blackpoint). The Linux figure is the strongest technical evidence of a shared codebase. [6][7][8][11][12]
Pillar 2: Credible
Code Overlap, Lynx to Sinobi
Sinobi shares 63.2% with Lynx and 55.9% with INC on binary comparison (SOCRadar). eSentire's TRU and Blackpoint independently flagged the overlap at Sinobi's emergence; Barracuda and Hive Pro concur. Single primary comparison, several concurring assessments. [19][20][21]
Pillar 3: Credible
Shared Personnel Signals
The INC and Lynx clear-web sites shared a registration email (BlackBerry, October 2024). In July 2026 SOCRadar observed a single operator, working from FortiBleed infrastructure, logged into both the INC and Lynx negotiation panels. The FBI and USSS wrote the brands as "INC/Lynx" in October 2026. Read together these point to marketing fronts over one operator and affiliate pool. [2][9][13]
Pillar 4: Credible
Older Heritage: Nemty, Karma, Nokoyawa
The researcher 3xp0rt linked the INC code seller "salfetka" to the actor "rinc" / "farnetwork", associated with Nokoyawa, JSWORM, Nefilim, Karma and Nemty. ACSC separately assesses that INC tradecraft shows significant overlap with Lynx and the historical Nemty, Nemty X, Karma and Nokoyawa operations. Two independent lines, neither one conclusive on its own. [1][23]

03

Operational Model

The Structural Difference: Who Holds the Money
Confirmed: two primary sources, one per brand

INC and Lynx differ on the single point that matters most for financial disruption. The ACSC advisory states that INC affiliates distribute the ransomware while "INC Ransom extorts affected organisations and handles payments": a franchise in which the operator owns the victim relationship and the wallet. Group-IB's January 2025 documentation of the Lynx affiliate panel shows the opposite: the Lynx affiliate controls the wallet and runs the negotiation, with the operator taking a 20% cut. This is the basis of the strongest leverage point in Section 11. [1][7]

INC: Franchise RaaS and Affiliate Migration

ACSC describes INC as a RaaS in which the operator develops the encryptor and payment portals and sells access, while affiliates perform the intrusion and deployment. Acronis characterises INC as semi-private and affiliate-based, with documented migration into it from Vice Society / Vanilla Tempest (2024) and from LockBit and BlackCat affiliates after those operations were disrupted. INC's revenue split, affiliate count and recruitment channel are not documented in open source. [1][14]

Lynx: The Best-Documented Panel

Group-IB's access to the Lynx affiliate panel documented five sections (News, Companies, Chats, Stuffers for sub-affiliates, and Leaks for scheduled publication), an 80/20 split in the affiliate's favour, a paid call-centre service for victim pressure, and affiliate-controlled wallets. Affiliates received a complete binary bundle (Windows, Linux x64, ARM, MIPS, ESXi) and mirror URLs through the News section. Recruitment ran on the RAMP forum under the handle "silencer" from August 2024, seeking experienced intrusion teams. [7][8]

Stated exclusion policy: Lynx claimed to avoid government, hospitals and non-profits beyond the standard CIS exclusion. Rapid7 characterised this as self-imposed risk management, and the lineage's own victim list (Easterseals Iowa, Lakelands Public Health, a Maryland county emergency-services department, the Tonga Ministry of Health) shows it was not an operational boundary. [1][10]
2026 Cross-Brand Developments
  • RAMP seized (28 January 2026). The FBI, with the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Florida and DOJ CCIPS, seized the RAMP forum's Tor and clear-web sites. RAMP was Lynx's documented recruitment venue; the seizure removed the operation's known front door for hiring without touching the operation itself. [5]
  • DeadLock (August 2026). Microsoft reported that one affiliate deploying the new Rust-based DeadLock encryptor was "previously associated with the Lynx and INC ransomware ecosystems", evidence that affiliates carry tooling across brands rather than belonging to one. [18]
  • Brokered access replaces own intrusion. Both documented 2026 supply lines (FortiBleed credentials, SonicWall SMA 1000 exploitation) are brokered, not INC's own work. See Section 04. [2][13][15][16]
Extortion and Negotiation

All three brands run double extortion with data exfiltrated before encryption, countdown-timer publication and progressive proof-of-breach releases. Negotiation detail in open source is thin: high-six to eight-figure opening demands scaled to victim size, steep discounts as deadlines approach, and no systematic DDoS or customer-harassment tier documented. In 2026 the INC extortion campaign used a clear-web contact domain and a US telephone number for pressure calls (see Section 11, point 6). [7][10][36]

04

Technical Capabilities

Encryption Implementation (all three brands)
  • Algorithm: AES-128 in CTR mode for file content; Curve25519 (donna) for key encapsulation
  • Recovery inhibition: Volume Shadow Copies deleted via VssAdmin; ESXi VM snapshots removed via vim-cmd
  • Wallpaper and printers: desktop wallpaper replaced; ransom note pushed to networked printers via StartDocPrinterW(), a behaviour uncommon among peer families
  • Extensions: .lynx (Lynx); INC and Sinobi use brand-specific extensions and note names
  • Crypto assessment: no disclosed design flaw; no public decryptor as of October 2026 (No More Ransom lists none of the three) [6][11][12]
2026 Capability Investment (INC side)
Credible: Acronis primary analysis, exposed-server findings corroborating

Acronis (June 2026) documented INC Windows and Linux/ESXi encryptors rewritten in Rust, an updated credential dumper targeting backup software, and bring-your-own-vulnerable-driver tooling to disable endpoint protection. Exposed INC affiliate servers analysed in June and July 2026 contained Linux encryptors compiled for an unusually wide range of architectures, including mainframe and other non-standard targets, indicating deliberate reach into heterogeneous enterprise and legacy environments. Lynx shows no new 2026 build in open source; the reference remains the multi-platform archive Group-IB documented in January 2025. Sinobi is Windows-only in public reporting. [7][14][19]

CIS Exclusion
Credible: consistent across Fortinet and Nextron; exact check logic not published

Lynx and INC binaries include system locale checks consistent with CIS exclusion. Combined with zero confirmed CIS victims across all three brands, this is a standard marker of a Russian-speaking operation avoiding domestic victims. [11][12]

Infrastructure and Hosting

Leak-site and negotiation infrastructure is Tor-based with clear-web mirrors, organised in tiers (public blog, victim negotiation, affiliate panel, file server). INC runs dual leak-site onions plus a payment onion with backend mirrors; the primary INC leak onion was reachable on 18 October 2026. Lynx operated a four-tier layout (admin, guest, corporate chat, blog) with roughly seven to eight mirrors each; most are down. Sinobi replicated the Lynx layout and is entirely offline. Where open source has attributed hosting and transport, it concentrates in known Russia-nexus providers: [19][24][31]

  • Aeza (AS210644): OFAC-sanctioned bulletproof host (1 July 2025); carried exposed INC affiliate staging servers in mid 2026, after designation
  • Railnet / Virtualine: reported as a legal front for a Russia-based bulletproof provider; carried Lynx affiliate remote-access traffic in late 2025 (The DFIR Report)
  • F.N.S Holdings (AS206092): VPN provider; source network for SonicWall SMA 1000 exploitation attributed to INC
  • Global Connectivity Solutions (AS215540): transit provider appearing both as a Sinobi exfiltration destination and in the SonicWall exploitation activity

No vendor has published the leak sites' hosting IPs, Tor guard or relay data, or TLS fingerprints. Full indicator sets are carried in the two government advisories and the vendor reports cited; this page does not reproduce them.

Decryptor Availability
No public decryptor exists for INC, Lynx or Sinobi as of October 2026, and no cryptographic flaw has been disclosed. Recovery without backups or operator cooperation is not feasible.

Top Defensive Priorities

Financial Infrastructure

Payment Model by Brand

INC collects ransom centrally: affiliates deploy, the operator extorts and is paid (ACSC). That makes the INC payment path a single chokepoint rather than a per-affiliate spray. Lynx did the reverse, with affiliate-controlled wallets and negotiation (Group-IB). Sinobi's model is undocumented. Bitcoin is the documented payment currency; Monero support is mentioned in secondary reporting only. [1][7]

On-Chain Analysis
Key intelligence gap: no public blockchain-analytics firm (Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic, Crystal) has published named wallet attribution, payment totals or laundering routes for INC, Lynx or Sinobi as of October 2026. Ransomwhere carries no entries for any of the three. This page asserts no on-chain attribution.

Mid-market to enterprise across all three brands, consistent with Group-IB's description of affiliates pursuing high-value corporate targets and with the MSP targeting posture Acronis identified. The multi-architecture Linux builds found on INC affiliate servers in 2026 indicate deliberate reach into legacy and heterogeneous enterprise estates. [7][14][25]

10

Trajectory Assessment

Operational Tempo

Measured at cluster level the operation is at scale and uncontested; measured at brand level it has concentrated. Lynx and Sinobi both peaked in January 2026 and then stopped; INC absorbed the tempo and has posted daily into mid October 2026. Four-plus years of cluster activity carry zero enforcement exposure beyond two advisories. [31][32]

Rebranding Signals
Analyst Inference: consolidation is the best fit, not a stated fact

Whether Lynx and Sinobi dormancy is consolidation, pause or quiet rebrand is unresolved. No vendor has announced a shutdown; no new family on the same codebase has been reported. If this is consolidation onto one brand, the operation is currently concentrated, which is the opposite of the resilience that rebrand-and-disperse usually buys. The measurable signal of the alternative is the emergence of a new family that BinDiffs against the INC/Lynx reference set.

Capability Evolution

Investment has moved to the INC side: Rust rewrites of the Windows and Linux/ESXi encryptors, a backup-software credential dumper, BYOVD defence evasion, and Linux builds for non-standard architectures. Lynx's affiliate-facing improvements (granular runtime options, call-centre service, multi-platform bundle) have not been updated in 2026. [7][14]

Structural Leverage Points

This section records where the operation's structure is exposed, on open-source evidence only, and what observable change would show that a given dependency has been degraded. Every point is tied to a cited fact and a measurable indicator. It describes exposure; it does not assign tasks. Priority order reflects impact against reversibility.

Point 1: highest value
INC's operator-controlled payment channel
Exposure: unlike Lynx, INC collects ransom centrally; affiliates deploy, the operator extorts and is paid. One payment path serves every INC victim.
Evidence: ACSC / NZ NCSC / CERT Tonga advisory, 6 March 2026 [1]; contrast Group-IB on Lynx [7].
Indicator: inbound consolidation from many victims to a small operator-controlled address set; a change in INC listing rate or negotiation behaviour following any off-ramp action.
Confidence: CONFIRMED on the model; wallet attribution is not public.
Point 2
Two identifiable initial-access suppliers
Exposure: INC's 2026 volume rides on brokered FortiBleed credentials and the SonicWall SMA 1000 zero-day cluster. Neither is INC's own work.
Evidence: FBI / USSS advisory, 6 October 2026 [2]; Rapid7, Resecurity, Volexity on SMA 1000 [15][16][37]; CISA KEV [3].
Indicator: INC listing rate against patch and remediation milestones on those two vectors; share of INC intrusions with a Fortinet or SonicWall entry point in vendor case reporting.
Confidence: CONFIRMED.
Point 3
Sanctioned and fronted hosting
Exposure: INC affiliate staging servers sat on Aeza after its OFAC designation; Lynx affiliate access ran through Railnet / Virtualine; SonicWall exploitation came from F.N.S Holdings and Global Connectivity Solutions ranges. None of these hosting ties has been actioned in connection with the lineage.
Evidence: The DFIR Report [24]; SOCRadar and exposed-server analysis [13][19]; OFAC Aeza designation [33].
Indicator: reachability and migration churn of leak-site and affiliate infrastructure; provider mix of attacker IPs in subsequent advisories.
Confidence: CREDIBLE (vendor attribution of hosting); the Aeza designation itself is CONFIRMED.
Point 4
The named, unactioned individual
Exposure: a government advisory has publicly named a Russia-based infrastructure controller for an INC incident. No charge, sanction or reward follows. This is the most advanced public attribution in the lineage and it currently carries no consequence.
Evidence: ACSC / NZ NCSC / CERT Tonga advisory [1].
Indicator: any designation, indictment or reward listing that cites the advisory; any change in exfiltration infrastructure patterns in later INC incidents.
Confidence: CONFIRMED.
Point 5
The brand-consolidation moment
Exposure: Lynx and Sinobi are dormant while INC runs hot. If this is consolidation, the operation is concentrated on one brand, one payment path and one leak site, the opposite of the dispersal that rebranding normally buys.
Evidence: tracker data [31]; no vendor statement of shutdown or rebrand.
Indicator: emergence of a new family on the same codebase (BinDiff against the INC/Lynx reference set); reactivation of Lynx or Sinobi sites.
Confidence: ANALYST INFERENCE.
Point 6
Western-reachable extortion plumbing
Exposure: the 2026 INC extortion campaign used a clear-web contact domain registered through a reachable registrar on Cloudflare nameservers, and a US telephone number for victim pressure calls. Low impact on the core, but inside Western provider reach and existing authorities.
Evidence: vendor incident reporting, 2026 [36].
Indicator: turnover of contact domains and numbers in ransom notes; registrar and nameserver mix over time.
Confidence: CREDIBLE.
Reading this section: points 1, 2 and 4 are documented by government advisories and are the structural facts of the operation. Point 3 rests on vendor hosting attribution. Points 5 and 6 are the analyst's read of the current moment and of the lowest-cost surfaces. None of this page's content draws on closed holdings.
