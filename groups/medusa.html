01

Executive Summary and Group Overview

Medusa is a vetted, closed-recruitment ransomware-as-a-service operation that the FBI dates to June 2021 and that reached high-volume, name-and-shame scale in early 2023 with the launch of its Tor-hosted leak site, the Medusa Blog. As of the 18 August 2026 update to joint advisory AA25-071A, the FBI, CISA and HHS attribute more than 500 victims to Medusa developers and affiliates through April 2026, up from the 300-plus figure reported in the original March 2025 version.

The operation is characterised by two things above all: exceptional speed against newly disclosed vulnerabilities in internet-facing management software, and a complete absence of law enforcement consequence. In five years of operation Medusa has produced no arrests, no indictments, no sanctions, no named individuals and no infrastructure seizure. That record is the single most analytically significant feature of the group and is treated as a finding in its own right in Section 08.

Naming discipline, three separate collisions. Medusa is not MedusaLocker. It is not the Medusa Android banking trojan. And it is unrelated to the US Department of Justice's Operation MEDUSA, the May 2023 disruption of the FSB's Snake malware network, which is a codename collision only. The FBI states in AA25-071A that Medusa is "unrelated to the MedusaLocker variant and the Medusa mobile malware variant," and Symantec separately states that Spearwing "is not believed to have any link" to MedusaLocker. Conflation of these four things is the most common error in secondary reporting on this group.
CONFIRMED. Medusa is a distinct ransomware family and operation, separate from MedusaLocker. Two independent authorities state this on the record: the FBI in AA25-071A and Symantec's Threat Hunter Team.

02

Lineage and Organizational Heritage

No source identifies a confirmed predecessor group from which Medusa descends, whether in code, personnel or infrastructure. MITRE records that Medusa began as a closed ransomware operation before evolving into a full RaaS by 2023. That is an internal structural change, not a lineage claim tied to another named brand. Medusa is therefore best treated as an independent-origin node rather than a descendant of the Conti or Hive trees that dominate the rest of the Russian-speaking ecosystem.

Founding-date conflict, and how to reconcile it. The FBI dates Medusa to June 2021. Symantec dates Spearwing's activity to 2023. Ransomware.live records its first tracked victim on 11 January 2023. The most economical reconciliation, and the one this profile adopts, is that the encryptor and a low-volume closed operation existed from mid-2021, and that the RaaS model, affiliate recruitment and the Medusa Blog all arrived together in early 2023. Vendors dating the group to 2023 are dating the leak site, not the malware.

Forward Heritage: The Storm-1175 Departure

The best-documented lineage event in Medusa's history runs forwards, not backwards. Around 9 August 2026 Microsoft Threat Intelligence reported that Storm-1175, an affiliate it had tracked deploying Medusa since 2023, had begun deploying a previously undocumented locker called StormEncryptor. Microsoft stated that this "marks the threat actor's first activity observed by Microsoft Threat Intelligence since April 2026, and a shift away from Medusa ransomware."

StormEncryptor is a distinct C++ binary rather than a Medusa fork. It appends .encrypted, drops a note named !!!README_FIRST!!!.txt into every scanned directory, and gives victims three days to make contact. Its emergence tracks closely with exploitation of CVE-2026-18577 in N-able N-central, which suggests the affiliate carried its infrastructure-targeting logic intact across the brand change.

CREDIBLE, single vendor. The Storm-1175 to StormEncryptor pivot rests entirely on Microsoft telemetry. BleepingComputer, The Hacker News, Security Affairs, SecurityWeek and SC Media all republished it, but republication is not corroboration. No second vendor has independently confirmed the actor continuity between the Medusa-affiliate phase and StormEncryptor.

The analytical significance is that a long-tenured, technically sophisticated affiliate cluster left the Medusa structure entirely, taking with it accumulated tradecraft, victim-network access intelligence and a demonstrated zero-day pipeline, while shedding the revenue-sharing and negotiation-control constraints of the affiliate relationship. It is an affiliate defection, not a rebrand of Medusa itself, and the distinction should be preserved rigorously.

03

Operational Model

Medusa runs a vetted, closed-recruitment RaaS. Affiliates are screened before being granted platform access, a departure from the open recruitment used by some competing brands, and are recruited through Russian-language cybercriminal forums where Medusa advertises alongside other RaaS operators. The group also buys access rather than relying solely on affiliate-driven intrusion: CISA documents payments to initial access brokers ranging from $100 to $1,000,000, and MITRE maps the behaviour to T1650, Acquire Access.

Per CISA, affiliates are "granted varying levels of trust based upon experience and profitability," and for less experienced affiliates the core developers retain control of ransom negotiations. That is a meaningful structural detail: negotiation control, not just tooling, is the developers' retained asset.

DO NOT PUBLISH A REVENUE SPLIT. The frequently repeated figure of a roughly 60/40 affiliate-to-developer split for Medusa traces only to low-quality aggregator content and its downstream copies. CISA states no percentage. Symantec states no percentage. Halcyon refers only to "substantial ransom portions." The Medusa affiliate split is an open intelligence gap, and the widely circulated 60 percent figure should be treated as unsourced. Note that MedusaLocker's publicly documented 55 to 60 percent affiliate share belongs to a different and unrelated group and is very likely the origin of the contamination.
Extortion Mechanics and Negotiation Playbook
  • 48-hour clock. Initial contact demands a victim response within 48 hours via a Tor-based live chat portal or the Tox encrypted messenger. Operators escalate to direct phone and email contact if the victim stays silent.
  • Public countdown. The victim's leak-site page carries a visible countdown timer and a page-view counter, alongside the ransom amount and a direct link to a Bitcoin wallet.
  • Paid delay. Victims can pay $10,000 in cryptocurrency to add a single day to the countdown. This is confirmed in the CISA advisory and is one of the more distinctive monetisation features of the brand.
  • Three payment paths. Pay for a decryption key, pay for deletion of exfiltrated data without decryption, or pay to extend the timer.
  • Credible follow-through. Medusa has generally published data on non-paying victims, which is what makes the threat operative rather than a bluff.
Triple extortion. CISA's advisory documents a case in which a victim paid, and was then contacted by a separate Medusa actor claiming the negotiator had stolen the payment, with a further demand for half the original amount to deliver "the real decryptor." Payment therefore does not reliably terminate the extortion relationship. This should be a standing caveat in any Medusa incident-response guidance.
The Publicity Apparatus: OSINT Without Borders

Medusa operates an unusually developed clear-web media function. CyberCX Cyber Intelligence assessed in March 2023, with high confidence, that the outlet branded "OSINT Without Borders" operates as the public relations function of the Medusa team, reporting on breaches and republishing stolen data.

CONFIRMED by evidence chain, single researcher. CyberCX's assessment rests on four linked observations: the Medusa leak site's Telegram button points to a channel branded OSINT Without Borders; a Medusa ransom note obtained by CyberCX explicitly links back to that outlet; a BreachForums user, t0mas, posted a review of data belonging to a victim listed on the Medusa leak site and uses the same profile image as the channel, claiming ownership of it; and t0mas's forum profile claims ownership of the osintcorp domain plus matching Facebook and Twitter personas. Halcyon and Bitdefender echo the clear-web presence independently.

The domain has since migrated from osintcorp.uk to osintcorp.net. As of August 2026 that domain is the only Medusa-associated asset still reachable, sitting at 100 percent uptime while every Tor mirror is down. That asymmetry is a collection opportunity and is treated as such in Section 10.

04

Technical Capabilities

Medusa affiliates weaponise newly disclosed vulnerabilities in internet-facing management software faster than almost any peer operation. CISA records exploitation within 24 hours of public disclosure, and Microsoft documents at least three cases in which Storm-1175 was exploiting a flaw before it was publicly disclosed at all.

A genuine source conflict, stated rather than smoothed over. Microsoft attributes multiple zero-days to Storm-1175. CISA's August 2026 update states there is "no indication Medusa actors develop their own zero-day or N-day vulnerabilities." These are reconcilable if one distinguishes acquiring an exploit from developing one, and an individual affiliate from the operation as a whole. They should not be presented as a single account.

Encryption Scheme

The most rigorous public analysis is SecurityScorecard's instruction-level reverse-engineering walkthrough. Medusa uses AES-256 in CBC mode through the Windows CNG/BCrypt interface, generating a fresh 32-byte key per file while reusing a single hardcoded 16-byte initialisation vector across all files. The AES key is wrapped with an RSA public key embedded in the binary, Base64-decoded and imported as an RSA CSP public key blob. Encrypted files carry a footer of ciphertext, the literal string MEDUSA, the file length, the RSA-encrypted AES key and a company identifier hash. The binary self-deletes on completion.

Correction to commonly published claims. The figure "RSA-2048" appears throughout secondary reporting on Medusa but is not established by any public reverse-engineering source. SecurityScorecard's walkthrough does not state the modulus size. This profile writes the scheme as AES-256 plus RSA of unestablished key size. Reporting that describes Medusa as using ChaCha20 is incorrect.
Kill Chain Toolset
  • Payload delivery. PDQ Deployer and PDQ Inventory push the encryptor gaze.exe across the estate. Symantec found the same deployment path in roughly two thirds of the Medusa intrusions it investigated. CISA also documents BigFix. Microsoft observed PDQ launching RunFileCopy.cmd.
  • EDR destruction. Symantec states that "in almost all Medusa attacks, KillAV and associated vulnerable drivers are used." Documented bring-your-own-vulnerable-driver tooling includes the ABYSSWORKER driver (smuol.sys), POORTRY (mbaer.sys), the STONESTOP loader, a repackaged ThrottleStop driver, wnbios.sys against Defender, and EDRSandblast. CISA maps this to T1685. This is arguably Medusa's most operationally distinctive capability and is under-represented in most public profiles.
  • Exfiltration. Rclone, frequently renamed (Symantec and Microsoft share the hash for a copy renamed lsp.exe), with Bandizip for staging. The August 2026 CISA update adds a notable refinement: Rclone planted inside folders already excluded from Microsoft Defender scanning, mapped to T1564.012.
  • Backup targeting. Microsoft alone documents a script used to recover passwords from Veeam backup software, enabling deployment to further connected systems. Veeam services also appear throughout the encryptor's service-stop list. Attribute this specifically to Microsoft; neither CISA nor Symantec repeats it.
  • Credential access and movement. Mimikatz, PsExec, Impacket, LSASS dumping, Advanced IP Scanner, SoftPerfect NetScan, RoboCopy.
  • Command and control. Cloudflared tunnels (renamed conhost.exe), Ligolo, RevSocks, Fast Reverse Proxy.
  • Living off remote management. AnyDesk, Atera, Level, N-able, DWAgent, MeshAgent, SimpleHelp, ScreenConnect, eHorus and Splashtop, which is what makes Medusa activity blend into legitimate IT administration.
  • Batch scripting. openrdp.bat, newuser.bat, removesophos.bat, duooff.bat, StopAllProcess.bat. Anti-forensics via vssadmin delete shadows and wevtutil cl.
Linux and ESXi Capability
CONFIRMED, and newly documented. The August 2026 CISA update establishes a Linux and hypervisor capability that earlier profiles of this group treated as unconfirmed. Medusa actors do not use PDQ Deploy in Linux environments. They transfer the encryptor, gaze.py, over SFTP and execute it with nohup python gaze.py &. They also abuse an ESXi host profile to change root passwords on Linux machines, mapped to the newly added technique T1675, ESXi Administration Command. Additional Linux tradecraft in the update includes a bash reverse shell over TCP 4444, GSocket, and a file_save.php webshell.

Analysts should note that blog content claiming Medusa uses esxcli to power off virtual machines and encrypt VMDK files at rest is not supported by any primary source located during this review, and reads as machine-generated. Use the CISA formulation.

CIS Exclusion: A Claim This Profile Removes
NOT SUPPORTED. Many profiles of Medusa assert that the binary carries a CIS kill switch, checking keyboard layout or system locale and halting on a Russian or CIS match. No reverse-engineering report documents such a check in the Medusa encryptor. SecurityScorecard's function-by-function walkthrough covers startup, argument parsing, key setup and enumeration and describes no locale, GetKeyboardLayoutList or GetUserDefaultLangID call. Symantec's behavioural analysis shows none either. Given that the walkthrough is comprehensive, the absence is meaningful negative evidence rather than a gap. The Russian-nexus assessment in Section 09 rests on forum behaviour and targeting patterns, not on a technical kill switch.

Top Defensive Priorities
  • Compress the patch window on internet-facing management software. Medusa's entire access model is built on the gap between disclosure and patching. GoAnywhere MFT, ScreenConnect, N-central, SmarterMail, TeamCity, FortiClient EMS, Mirth Connect, Ivanti and CrushFTP are the specific product families to prioritise. A 24-hour exploitation cadence means a 30-day patch SLA is not a defence.
  • Audit Defender and EDR exclusion paths as an active threat surface. The August 2026 finding that Medusa stages tooling inside existing exclusions turns a hygiene item into a detection control.
  • Enforce driver blocklisting and monitor for kernel-level EDR tampering. Given that vulnerable drivers appear in almost all Medusa intrusions, Microsoft's vulnerable driver blocklist and alerting on driver-load anomalies are load-bearing, not optional.
  • Inventory and constrain RMM agents. Any RMM not on an approved list should generate an alert on install. This is the single highest-yield detection for Medusa post-compromise activity.
  • Isolate and credential-separate backup infrastructure, including Veeam. Medusa targets backups before encryption specifically to remove the recovery option.
  • Extend all of the above to Linux and ESXi. The Linux encryptor path is confirmed as of August 2026; Windows-only controls now leave a documented gap.
  • Plan for payment not ending the incident. The documented triple-extortion pattern means recovery planning should not assume a paid ransom closes the matter.

Financial Infrastructure

Medusa's financial profile is defined by an absence. For a RaaS operation with more than 500 confirmed victims across five years, the public record contains no wallet clustering, no laundering pathway, no identified cash-out point and no credible total-revenue estimate.

CONFIRMED ABSENCE, and this is a finding. No Medusa-specific on-chain analysis has been published by TRM Labs, Chainalysis or Elliptic. Cyfirma tracks Medusa in its monthly ransomware reporting but publishes no wallet clustering. RansomLook's cryptocurrency module returns "no wallet found for this group." Chainalysis's 2026 Crypto Crime Report carries an ecosystem-wide ransomware total but no Medusa line item. Compare this to the depth of tracing available for LockBit, Conti or 8Base, where traced sums, exchange endpoints and named launderers are all on the record.

Sanctions Exposure

No OFAC action names Medusa, Spearwing, Storm-1175, FROZEN SPIDER or any individual tied to the operation. Recent Treasury activity in this space has instead targeted shared ecosystem infrastructure: the November 2025 trilateral action against the Russia-based bulletproof hosting provider Media Land, whose disclosed ransomware clients were LockBit, BlackSuit and Play and did not include Medusa, and the July 2026 designation of a VPN service and a cryptor seller.

ANALYST INFERENCE. Medusa's absence from every sanctions instrument despite its victim volume most likely reflects the same underlying condition as the on-chain gap: nobody has published the financial attribution work that a designation would normally rest on. Whether that reflects resourcing, prioritisation, or work held below the open-source line cannot be distinguished from available reporting.

A State Actor as Customer: Lazarus Group
CREDIBLE, single vendor, and smaller than commonly reported. Symantec and Carbon Black reported on 24 February 2026 that North Korea's Lazarus Group had used the Medusa encryptor. The evidence is tool co-presence: Lazarus-exclusive tooling, principally the Comebacker backdoor, alongside Blindingcan and a custom RP_Proxy, in intrusions where Medusa was deployed. This is tool-overlap attribution, not infrastructure or victim-based attribution.

The scope is two incidents: one successful Medusa deployment against an unnamed Middle East target, and one failed attempt against a US healthcare organisation. Symantec explicitly could not attribute all four of the US healthcare and non-profit victims listed since November 2025 to DPRK activity, and could not resolve which Lazarus sub-group was responsible, noting that the tradecraft resembles Stonefly or Andariel while Comebacker also maps to Pompilus. Figures to avoid. Claims that Lazarus conducted "366 incidents" using Medusa at an average of $260,000 conflate three separate numbers. The 366 figure is Symantec's count of Medusa leak-site claims overall. The $260,000 average covers four healthcare and non-profit victims, only one of which is attributed to Lazarus. The DPRK-attributed activity is two incidents.

The headline finding of this profile. Across five years and more than 500 confirmed victims, Medusa has generated no arrests, no indictments, no named individuals, no OFAC designations, no Europol or NCA action, no infrastructure seizure and no domain takedown. The Regulatory Response That Did Occur

Government engagement with Medusa has been entirely advisory rather than coercive. The March 2025 joint advisory and its August 2026 update are substantive documents, and the addition of HHS as a co-author in 2026 signals escalating concern about healthcare exposure. But an advisory imposes cost on defenders, not on the operation. Nothing in the public record has raised Medusa's cost of doing business.

ANALYST INFERENCE, competing explanations. Three readings are consistent with the evidence and cannot be separated from open sources. First, an attribution gap: the absence of financial tracing (Section 06) means the evidentiary foundation for a designation or indictment may simply not exist. Second, prioritisation: Medusa lacks the political salience of Colonial Pipeline or the scale of LockBit, and enforcement attention has demonstrably followed salience. Third, operational security: the core operators may be genuinely harder to identify than their peers, with the leak-site persona work handled through the OSINT Without Borders layer while the developers stay dark. The first explanation is the most economical, since it also explains the on-chain gap, but confidence is low.
09

Attribution and State Nexus

Assessed Jurisdiction

Medusa's core operators are assessed to be Russian-speaking, on behavioural and circumstantial grounds rather than direct evidence. The supporting indicators are: recruitment and victim-update posting on Russian-language cybercrime forums; use of Fenya, the Russian criminal argot, by affiliates seeking credibility on those forums; Cyrillic script in operational tooling; and systematic avoidance of targets in Russia and the CIS.

CREDIBLE, low to moderate confidence. Cyjax, which developed the forum-behaviour case, explicitly qualifies it: "we cannot be certain that this is a Russian hacking operation." No leaked communications, named personnel, government statement or infrastructure geolocation supports the assessment. As set out in Section 04, the CIS-exclusion indicator here is a targeting pattern, not a technical kill switch, because no reverse-engineering source documents a locale check in the binary.
Russian Intelligence Services Nexus

No confirmed or officially assessed relationship exists between Medusa and the FSB, SVR or GRU. There is no documented safe-harbour arrangement, no non-prosecution understanding, and no intelligence-sharing relationship in the public record. Medusa's evidentiary position is markedly weaker than the comparison cases: Conti's leaked internal chats produced direct alignment indicators, and US government statements have addressed Russian safe harbour for LockBit explicitly. Nothing equivalent exists here.

CIS-target avoidance is a near-universal feature of Russian-speaking ransomware operations and functions as self-preservation rather than as evidence of coordination. Applied to Medusa it supports an assessment of Russian-speaking origin at low to moderate confidence and supports no state-nexus conclusion at all.

Three Geographies, Kept Separate
This profile contains three distinct geographic claims operating at three different organisational layers. Conflating them is the most likely analytical error a reader will make.
  • Core operators, assessed Russian-speaking. Behavioural evidence, low to moderate confidence. This is the jurisdiction claim about Medusa itself.
  • Storm-1175, described as China-based. An affiliate-level claim, and a fragile one. It originates in a single Microsoft Threat Intelligence social-media post from April 2024; every subsequent instance links back to that post. Microsoft's own detailed April 2026 blog on Storm-1175 does not repeat it, describing the actor only as a financially motivated cybercriminal threat actor. BleepingComputer hedges it as "believed to be." Microsoft has also held this actor at a temporary Storm designation for three years, which is itself a statement about attribution confidence. Microsoft consistently characterises Storm-1175 as financially motivated, never as state-sponsored: this is not a PRC state-nexus claim.
  • Lazarus Group, DPRK state actor, as customer. A North Korean state-sponsored group using the Medusa encryptor in two incidents. This is a customer relationship running into Medusa, not a state relationship behind it, and it says nothing about the operators' jurisdiction.

A China-based affiliate operating inside a Russian-speaking RaaS is unusual but not contradictory. Affiliate rosters are geographically mixed by design, and affiliate origin carries no implication for operator origin.

Summary Assessment
No confirmed state nexus of any kind exists for Medusa's core operation. The Russian-speaking assessment rests on forum behaviour and targeting patterns, not on attribution evidence. A confirmed DPRK state-actor customer relationship exists at single-vendor confidence and is analytically distinct from the operator-jurisdiction question. The China descriptor attached to Storm-1175 is an affiliate-level claim from one vendor, unrepeated in that vendor's own long-form reporting, and should not be carried into statements about Medusa.
10

Trajectory Assessment

Medusa is in the most uncertain phase of its five-year existence. The operation has not announced a shutdown, has not rebranded, and has not been disrupted. It has simply stopped posting.

Dormancy Indicators
  • No new leak-site victim since April 2026. Reported by The Record on 18 August 2026. This should be attributed to The Record's reporting and not to the CISA advisory, which does not contain the statement.
  • Every Tor mirror offline. Both ransomware.live and RansomLook record all Medusa .onion addresses as down. Ransomware.live flagged the group inactive at roughly 188 days as of this build.
  • Storm-1175 last observed on Medusa in April 2026, the same month the leak-site postings stop, before resurfacing in August 2026 with its own locker.
  • osintcorp.net still up at 100 percent. The publicity layer outlived the extortion infrastructure, which is not the pattern of a deliberate wind-down.
CREDIBLE trend signal, not a confirmed collapse. Leak-site silence is a lagging and noisy indicator. Postings trail intrusions, and a negotiation-driven model can run privately for months without publishing. The convergence of four independent signals on the same April 2026 boundary is what raises this above noise.
Competing Explanations
Hypothesis A
Affiliate attrition
Storm-1175's departure removed a high-tempo affiliate that may have generated a substantial share of visible output. If Medusa's leak-site volume was concentrated in a small number of productive affiliates, one defection would produce exactly this signature. The timing alignment is the strongest evidence.
Hypothesis B
Heat avoidance
The University of Mississippi Medical Center attack drew significant law enforcement attention according to experts cited by The Record. A deliberate go-quiet following an incident that attracted federal focus on a US hospital is a recognised pattern. Note there is no evidence of an actual investigation reaching the group.
Hypothesis C
Quiet reconstitution
The brand is dark but the operators are unidentified, unsanctioned and unindicted. Nothing prevents reappearance under a new name. The surviving clear-web publicity asset would be the natural place for a relaunch to surface, which is why it is worth monitoring.
Hypothesis D
Collection artefact
If the Tor mirrors are down for infrastructure reasons, trackers would record silence whether or not the operation continues. The FBI's investigation-based count runs only to April 2026, so it cannot arbitrate. Weakest of the four, but not excluded.
Capability Trajectory

Nothing in the record suggests technical decline. Medusa's Linux and ESXi capability was formally documented for the first time in August 2026, meaning the confirmed capability envelope was still widening as the brand went quiet. Storm-1175's post-departure behaviour points the same way: a custom C++ locker built and fielded quickly, with exploitation of a freshly disclosed N-central flaw inside roughly two weeks of disclosure. Departing affiliates from this ecosystem lose a brand, not a skill set.

Standing Intelligence Gaps
  • No named individual anywhere in the operation, unlike every comparably prolific group.
  • No blockchain forensics from any named firm. This is the gap that most likely blocks enforcement action.
  • No established affiliate revenue split; the widely circulated 60/40 figure is unsourced.
  • No established RSA key size in the encryption scheme.
  • Whether the April 2026 silence reflects decline, deliberate dormancy, reconstitution or a collection artefact cannot be resolved from open sources.
  • Whether affiliate clusters beyond Storm-1175 exist under separate vendor designations. Darktrace references Medusa collaboration with big-game-hunting groups and IABs but supplies insufficient technical detail for confident inclusion.
Collection Priorities
  • osintcorp.net. The only live Medusa-associated asset and the most likely surface for any relaunch or successor announcement. Monitor content, hosting, registration and the associated Telegram channel.
  • Tor mirror status. A mirror returning would distinguish infrastructure failure from operational cessation.
  • The t0mas persona and successor accounts on BreachForums and equivalent venues, given the documented linkage to Medusa's publicity function.
  • New lockers appearing against the same product families (managed file transfer, RMM, mail servers) with a PDQ-style deployment pattern and a vulnerable-driver EDR killer. That combination is Medusa's operational signature and would survive a rebrand.
