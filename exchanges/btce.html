Overall Assessment
BTC-e (2011 to 2017) was the founding node of the Russian low-KYC exchange model that Garantex, Grinex, and today's post-Garantex network still follow. It cashed out 95% of tracked ransomware payments, laundered the Mt. Gox theft, and was seized by U.S. authorities in July 2017 because its servers sat in New Jersey. Its only direct organizational successor was WEX, launched 52 days later by BTC-e's own technical administrator using BTC-e's customer database, and destroyed within 14 months by an alleged FSB-linked expropriation of ~$450M and an exit-scam collapse. No later exchange, including Garantex, has a documented personnel, funds, or code inheritance from BTC-e/WEX. The lineage ended organizationally in 2018; what survived is the business model, the impunity pattern, and roughly $700M+ in still-moving operator-controlled wallets.

02

Lineage and Organizational Heritage

Key Finding : The Only Direct Successor Is WEX
WEX is the single documented organizational successor to BTC-e: same hidden administrator (Bilyuchenko), the entire BTC-e customer database and balances, a cloned interface, and treasury wallet continuity confirmed on-chain. The chain ends there. The planned second successor, Vladex, never launched. Claims that BTC-e personnel went on to found Garantex are not supported by any official document: the June 2023 DOJ statement that Bilyuchenko "went on to help set up the notorious BTC-e virtual currency exchange" refers to BTC-e itself and is frequently misquoted as referring to Garantex. [DOJ 2023, verified against full text]

BTC-e → WEX Successor Chain

July 2011
BTC-e launches, founded by Vinnik and Bilyuchenko. Grows into one of the "Big Four" exchanges, at one point handling ~2.5% of all bitcoin exchange volume. [DOJ, Bitcoin Wiki]
2011 to 2014
Mt. Gox theft proceeds flow in: of ~647,000 stolen BTC, ~530,000 went to accounts at three services (BTC-e, Trade Hill, Mt. Gox itself), with ~300,000 BTC to three BTC-e accounts ("Vamnedam", "Grmbit", "Petr") linked to Bilyuchenko and Verner. [SDNY indictment 2023, WizSec]
January 2017
Sealed 21-count superseding indictment returned in NDCA (3:16-cr-00227; original indictment 2016). Co-defendants beyond Vinnik and the BTC-e entity include Nikonorov, Golovanov, and Buyanov Single Source. [Court docket, Sammis Law]
July 25, 2017
Vinnik arrested in Ouranoupoli, Greece. U.S. agents seize BTC-e's New Jersey servers and the btc-e.com domain. FinCEN assesses $110,003,314 against BTC-e and $12M against Vinnik on July 26 to 27. [DOJ, FinCEN]
July 30 to 31, 2017
Days after the seizure, ~929.5 BTC and ~485,705 ETH move out of BTC-e-controlled addresses, "including to WEX" per the DOJ forfeiture record. Treasury continuity between the two platforms begins before WEX publicly exists. [DOJ complaint 2025, Dynamis]
September 15, 2017
WEX launches, announced through BTC-e's own Twitter account, 52 days after the takedown. All former BTC-e accounts and balances migrate (62% crypto / 38% "WEX tokens" representing frozen equity). Nominal owner: Vasilyev via World Exchange Services Pte. Ltd. (Singapore); actual owner per BBC: Bilyuchenko. Peak daily turnover ~$80M (RBC). [CoinDesk 2017, BBC/Meduza]
April 2018
Alleged FSB expropriation: Bilyuchenko testified he was taken to FSB offices in Moscow over three days and surrendered cold-wallet access; ~30,000 BTC and ~700,000 LTC (~$350M to $450M) left WEX wallets, money he was told would go to "the FSB Russia fund". Credible Single Source [BBC Russian Service 2019]
July 2018
Withdrawals halt; BTC trades on WEX at >$9,000 against a ~$6,400 global price (locked-exit premium). Ownership-transfer agreement signed July 21, 2018 with Dmitry Khavchenko ("Moryachok"), ex-DNR militiaman close to Malofeev. [Bitcoinist, ForkLog via Quadriga Initiative]
Late 2018
WEX collapses; ~$400M to $450M in customer assets lost. ~93,000 ETH (~$18M) from WEX cold wallets moves through Binance, which freezes two accounts. Domains drift (wex.fit, wex.in, wex.ink) then die. [Bitcoinist, Quadriga Initiative]
2019 to 2026
No third organizational reconstitution. Vladex (planned Malofeev-backed successor) never launches. Enforcement grinds on: Vasilyev arrests (2019 to 2024) ending in U.S. extradition June 2025; Bilyuchenko/Verner indictments 2023; Klimenka 2024; Vinnik plea 2024 and swap 2025; civil forfeiture filed June 2025, active into 2026. [DOJ, TASS, CourtListener]

Service Model and Business Operations

Exchange Mechanics

Confirmed Centralized spot exchange: BTC, LTC, and altcoins against USD, RUB, and EUR, with internal "code" instruments (transferable balance codes) that functioned as a bearer-value layer popular with cash-out brokers. Users could buy, sell, and store largely anonymously; accounts required only a username, password, and email address. [DOJ 2017, FinCEN]

Confirmed WEX replicated the product set one-for-one at launch, including trading pairs and the token-for-frozen-balance mechanism. [CoinDesk 2017]

KYC/AML : Stated Policy vs. Observed Behavior

Stated vs. Observed
BTC-e made essentially no public compliance claims; its site was seized in 2017 and no verbatim KYC policy text survives in available sourcing. This is therefore a documented failure profile rather than a claims-versus-reality gap: the exchange never registered with FinCEN, maintained no meaningful KYC or AML program, and marketed the resulting anonymity.

Fiat Rail Analysis (Mandatory)

Confirmed No conventional named banking relationships are documented for BTC-e; that absence was structural, not an intelligence gap in the ordinary sense. Fiat moved through shell companies and third-party processors rather than exchange-named bank accounts. The one hard documented fiat reservoir: Canton Business Corporation balances of ~USD $89.68M (plus AUD/RUB/GBP) held at FXOpen / XP Solutions Limited (New Zealand/Australia), restrained under a Dec 2019 NDCA warrant, subject of the June 2020 NZ Police NZ$140M restraint (largest in NZ Police history), and claimed again in the 2025 U.S. forfeiture complaint. [NZ Police, DOJ complaint 2025]

Credible Czech bank accounts of Nano ABC LP appear as additional defendant property in the 2025 complaint, indicating a further European fiat holding channel. [DOJ complaint 2025, Dynamis]

Day-to-day retail fiat rails (which processors and cash brokers moved customer RUB/USD in and out, 2011 to 2017) remain undocumented in official sources. Contrast with successors Garantex (Sberbank/Tinkoff/Alfa cards) where retail rails are well documented.

Licensing and Regulatory Standing

Confirmed No license in any jurisdiction at any time. FinCEN treated BTC-e as a foreign-located MSB doing U.S. business without registration; no EU/UK authorization existed for Always Efficient LLP, which filed dormant-style accounts under nominee officers until they resigned in September 2017. [FinCEN, Companies House]

Platform Continuity and Codebase

Confirmed WEX reproduced BTC-e's interface, feature set, and trading pairs and imported the customer database wholesale : the clearest platform-level continuity indicator in the lineage. [CoinDesk 2017, BBC]

Post-Disruption Resilience Assessment

Reconstitution Pattern : Origin of the Playbook
BTC-e wrote the first draft of the reconstitution playbook later perfected by Garantex/Grinex: pre-moving treasury funds within days of seizure, relaunching under a new brand within weeks on the same customer base, and issuing $1-pegged tokens against frozen balances. Unlike Garantex, the successor was not pre-positioned before disruption, and the model proved fragile: WEX survived 14 months and died from internal predation (the alleged FSB expropriation and exit-scam sale), not Western enforcement.

State Nexus Assessment

Four-Tier Classification : Assessed Tier
BTC-e operating period (2011 to 2017): NONE documented. WEX phase and aftermath (2018 to present): PROBABLE COOPERATION indicators, resting on a single-source account, plus confirmed post-hoc state protection of operators. The alleged April 2018 FSB expropriation, if accurate, is not "cooperation" in the safe-harbor sense but coercive state capture of criminal proceeds : the state as predator, not patron. It is scored conservatively because it rests on one underlying investigation.

BTC-e Period : Evidence of Absence

  • What a nexus would look like: hosting inside Russia, tip-offs ahead of Western action, tasking of the platform for state operations. None is documented. BTC-e hosted its core infrastructure in the United States, the single decision that enabled its destruction : inconsistent with state operational guidance. Confirmed (absence)
  • All disruption was Western (U.S. seizure, Greek arrest, FinCEN penalty). No Russian enforcement against BTC-e while it operated. Confirmed

WEX Phase and Aftermath : Elevation Indicators

  • Alleged FSB extraction of ~$450M from WEX (April 2018), per Bilyuchenko's testimony and BBC-authenticated audio implicating Malofeev's circle and serving FSB officers "Igor" and "Grigori". Credible Single Source
  • The planned successor "Vladex" was explicitly pitched as regulated infrastructure to "protect the financial and economic sovereignty of the Russian Federation" : state-aligned by design, though never launched. Credible [Meduza/BBC]
  • Post-hoc protection: Russia filed competing extradition requests for Vinnik (2017 to 2020), traded Fogel to repatriate him (Feb 2025), and keeps Bilyuchenko domestically prosecuted but unextradited; the MVD declined WEX victims' request to freeze moving funds (2021); the Supreme Court erased the 18B RUB civil claim (2025). Confirmed (swap, non-extradition) / Credible (court actions)
  • April 2024: Kommersant reported MVD cyber-unit officer Georgy Satyukov charged in absentia over ~5B RUB in crypto bribes for "patronizing" WEX : direct krysha allegation from Russia's own record. Credible Single Source

Negative Evidence : Against Direct Control

  • No document names any Russian state body as operator or owner of BTC-e or WEX at any point. Confirmed
  • The alleged 2018 FSB action was expropriation from the operators, implying the state did not control the platform beforehand. Analyst Inference

Trajectory Assessment

Market Position and Displacement

Confirmed BTC-e's 2017 seizure displaced its volume first into WEX (briefly the largest Russian-language exchange), then, after WEX's 2018 collapse, diffusely across the Russian-language exchange ecosystem. Garantex, founded 2019, filled the same market niche without organizational inheritance. The niche has never since been vacant: BTC-e → WEX → (gap) → Garantex → Grinex → post-Garantex network. [CoinDesk, Elliptic, TRM]

Disruption Impact

Structural Assessment
The BTC-e action remains the template case for what works and what does not. What worked: attacking the U.S. infrastructure footprint, the fiat reservoirs (NZ), and the operators' travel (Greece, Italy, Poland, Croatia, Latvia, India analogues later). What did not: the customer base, keys, and personnel survived intact inside Russia, reconstituting in 52 days. Terminal degradation ultimately came less from Western enforcement than from intra-Russian predation, the alleged 2018 FSB expropriation and exit-scam sale, an outcome that destroyed the platform but transferred, rather than removed, the criminal proceeds.
