Overall Assessment
001k (001k.bot / 001k.exchange) is a Ukraine-based, Telegram-native crypto-to-fiat exchange and physical cash-courier network that has operated since at least 2018 and, per Chainalysis data provided to ICIJ, received more than $14.8 billion in cryptocurrency since August 2022, overwhelmingly USDT. The defining analytical finding is a wide gap between a polished compliance narrative (mandatory KYC, sanctions screening, even a paid AML wallet-screening add-on) and observed behavior: in ICIJ's November 2025 "Coin Laundry" investigation a Toronto Star reporter completed a 2,000 USDT cash payout authenticated only by the serial number of a $5 banknote, with no identity document requested, and journalists in Quebec were offered cash deliveries up to roughly $1 million with no ID. The second finding is jurisdictional flight: the named operator of record shifted from a small, recently created Lithuanian company (CryptoBarrel UAB, two-employee, confirmed in Lithuanian registries) to a pseudonymous Panama shell (SATOSHI NAKAMOTO CORP.) in the T&C between earlier reporting and an April 2026 update, conspicuously timed after the ICIJ exposure. Despite high-volume flows, dense multi-jurisdictional scrutiny, and a "High Risk" rating from the AML Network watchdog, 001k carries no OFAC, EU, UK, or FinCEN designation and is not the subject of any public DOJ action. It was the catalyst for Canada's March 2026 FINTRAC revocation sweep but, being unregistered, was not itself a named subject of that enforcement. State nexus is assessed at NONE. Corrections to the source draft are folded into the relevant sections: the $14.8 billion figure is ICIJ citing Chainalysis (not a standalone Chainalysis publication); the $2.7 billion figure is an NGO estimate for the broader WhiteBIT scheme, not a 001k-specific number; the claim that the EU AML authority AMLA was "set to probe" the findings is not accurate (AMLA publicly stated it does not conduct such investigations); and the operator email on the June 2026 launch was support@001k.bot, not pr@001k.exchange.

Lineage and Organizational Heritage

Jurisdictional Flight, Not Predecessor / Successor

Key Finding
001k has no conventional exchange predecessor or successor. Its "lineage" is a chain of operator-of-record substitutions designed to keep the same product behind progressively more enforcement-resistant corporate shells. The 001k.exchange domain dates to October 2019, predating the Telegram bot domain 001k.bot (created April 2024) and the Lithuanian operating company CryptoBarrel UAB (registered March 2024), which indicates the brand was running before the formal corporate wrappers existed. The decisive move is the shift of the named operator from CryptoBarrel UAB (Lithuania, an EU VASP-eligible jurisdiction) to SATOSHI NAKAMOTO CORP. (Panama, no equivalent CASP regime), reflected in the April 2026 Terms & Conditions and conspicuously timed in the months after ICIJ's November 2025 exposure. 03

Service Model and Business Operations

Exchange Mechanics and Product Set

Credible 001k runs a bifurcated model. The first layer is an on-chain swap service via the Telegram bot (@OO1kBOT) and, since June 2026, a web dashboard (app.001k.bot): instant crypto-to-fiat swaps across 14-plus cryptocurrencies and multiple fiat currencies (USD, EUR, AED, UAH), limit orders, off-chain internal transfers, a custodial wallet, a three-level referral program, and API access for third-party integrations. The second layer is a physical cash-courier service: in-person cash payouts in 300-plus cities across 60-plus countries, plus card, IBAN and e-wallet off-ramps. A manager-led premium tier is self-described for transactions of $50,000 and up. This architecture (anonymous stablecoin inflow, centralized swap/layering, physical cash exit) is the classic integration-layering structure that AML practitioners flag. [5][9][18]

Stated KYC/AML Posture vs Observed Behavior

Stated Policy vs Observed Behavior (Schema Rule)
Stated policy (from the exchange's own materials): the AML & KYC Policy (published on Telegraph, last updated May 2026) asserts mandatory full KYC including biometric verification, government ID, proof of address and beneficial-ownership collection for legal entities; third-party identity verification; blockchain-analytics screening; ongoing sanctions screening against OFAC, EU and UN lists; and an explicit exclusion list (Iran, North Korea, Syria, Cuba, the occupied Ukrainian oblasts, Russia, Belarus and others). It even sells an on-demand AML wallet-screening add-on, marketing itself as a compliance-tool provider. The policy also prohibits "alternative settlement mechanisms... payment agents, compensation schemes, offsetting arrangements, netting," language that reads as an attempt to legally disclaim the very cash-courier and "rearrangement" services that define the product. Credible (the exchange's own claims) [21][24]

Observed behavior (sourced to investigative testing and analytics, not the exchange's claims): ICIJ's undercover test converted 2,000 USDT to cash in Toronto with no identity document, authenticated only by a photographed $5-banknote serial number; CBC reports roughly US$1,900 was paid out. Quebec journalists were offered cash deliveries up to about $1 million with no ID. AML Network and Crystal-cited analysis describe more than $14.8 billion in inflows with "little or no KYC enforcement." The gap between a sophisticated stated policy and a no-ID cash network is the primary analytical finding and indicates structural KYC/AML failure by design rather than lapse. Confirmed [1][3][5][9]
No law-enforcement charging document setting out 001k's specific KYC thresholds, Travel Rule handling, or monitoring gaps exists; the strongest primary evidence is ICIJ's undercover transaction, not a regulator's evidentiary file.

Fiat Rails (Schema Rule: Must Be Addressed)

Analyst Inference 001k advertises card payouts to Ukrainian, Russian and Chinese bank cards, IBAN transfers, and e-wallet rails (PayPal, Wise, Revolut), alongside physical cash. No specific bank or payment-processor relationship is documented in open sources; no named correspondent, acquirer, or processor appears in the reviewed reporting. The absence is itself a finding: a network advertising card disbursement to three countries' banking systems and e-wallets necessarily touches regulated payment rails, but the counterparties are undisclosed, consistent with deliberate opacity and reliance on money-mule card networks rather than a single banking relationship. The cash-courier leg is designed precisely to avoid named fiat-rail dependencies. [11][18]

Licensing and Regulatory Standing

Credible Through CryptoBarrel UAB, 001k could point to a Lithuanian corporate registration to imply EU/FCIS alignment, but the Lithuanian company is a small, recently created entity, not a substantive licensed VASP operation, and the registry does not tie it to 001k. With the operator of record now a Panama shell, there is no VASP/CASP licensing regime equivalent to the EU's MiCA framework, no Financial Intelligence Unit reporting obligation comparable to Lithuania's FCIS, and no Western authorization. Treat all compliance and licensing claims as marketing absent a primary regulator record. 001k holds no FINTRAC registration in Canada despite operating cash payouts there. [1][13][20]

The Compliance-Tool Paradox

Credible 001k markets an on-demand AML wallet-screening add-on, sold on a subscription or token basis, positioning itself as a compliance-tooling provider at the same time it operates a no-ID cash network. This dual posture is itself an infrastructure observation: the platform packages a veneer of compliance capability as a product feature while the core cash leg is built to avoid the controls that screening would imply. [12]

Resilience and Adaptability

Confirmed 001k's infrastructure is resilient by construction. The primary interface is a Telegram bot, which is hard to take down through app-store or hosting action; Cloudflare fronting and privacy-shielded, multi-registrar domains complicate attribution and seizure; and the corporate layer can be swapped (Lithuania to Panama) without touching the technical stack. Rather than contracting after the ICIJ exposure, 001k expanded, launching a full web dashboard in June 2026. [6][20][31]

Three-Phase On-Chain Flow (001k as Node)

Phase 1: Receipt

Confirmed 001k receives high-volume stablecoin inflows (primarily USDT) into a network of receiving wallets. ICIJ characterizes the pattern as consistent with a cash-out service: incoming stablecoins, rapid onward movement, and no conventional customer profiling. Funds enter from anonymous or lightly screened wallets and from other exchangers using 001k as a payout rail. [1][5]

Phase 2: Layering

Confirmed Inflows move rapidly onward to customer accounts at major centralized exchanges (Binance, OKX, Kraken, WhiteBIT), where they co-mingle with general exchange liquidity, and through 001k's internal/off-chain transfer and custodial-wallet features. The use of multiple exchanges and accounts to obscure origin is the documented layering mechanism. Settlement is USDT-heavy, consistent with the broader cash-desk ecosystem. Credible (mechanism) [1][5]

Phase 3: Extraction

Confirmed The defining extraction leg is physical cash, delivered by courier in 300-plus cities, plus card/IBAN/e-wallet off-ramps. The no-ID cash handoff (authenticated by a banknote serial number) is the point at which value leaves the traceable on-chain environment, which is what makes the network a "money-laundering nightmare" in investigators' words. [1][3]

State Nexus Tier: NONE

Assessment
Why NONE: there is no documented state ownership, tasking, coordination, or protection of 001k by Ukraine or any other state. 001k presents as a privately operated, profit-driven cash-out network, not a state instrument. Its principal counterparty, WhiteBIT, was declared an "undesirable organization" by Russia in January 2026 for supporting Ukraine's military, which weighs strongly against any Russian state nexus for the 001k/WhiteBIT cluster.

What would be expected if a higher tier existed, and is absent: no evidence of intelligence-service tasking, no documented protection from prosecution, no state-linked ownership, and no pattern of selective enforcement immunity tied to state direction. On the contrary, the ecosystem faces NGO-reported NABU and SBI/DBR interest in Ukraine and an international search around a 001k principal, which is inconsistent with active state protection.

Caveat (low confidence): 001k continues to operate openly from an assessed Ukrainian base despite high-profile ICIJ exposure, which could be read as passive tolerance or simply weak/slow domestic enforcement during wartime. This is logged as a low-confidence signal, not a basis to escalate to TOLERATED SAFE HARBOR, because there is no evidence the state is aware-and-deliberately-refraining as opposed to under-resourced. Analyst Inference [10][11][13]

Negative Evidence (Expected if Higher Nexus Existed)

If 001k were under TOLERATED SAFE HARBOR, PROBABLE COOPERATION, or DIRECT CONTROL, one would expect documented state coordination, demonstrable protection from enforcement, or ownership ties to state organs. None are present. The available record points the other way: investigative and NGO reporting describes criminal-justice interest in the wider ecosystem, and the jurisdictional flight to a Panama shell is the behavior of an operator hardening against enforcement, not one relying on state cover. The assessment holds at NONE, with the open-operation-despite-exposure caveat flagged as the key uncertainty. Analyst Inference

Disruption Impact

Structural Assessment
To date there has been no disruptive action: no sanction, indictment, seizure, or takedown. The only pressure is reputational (ICIJ exposure) and indirect (the Canadian FINTRAC sweep that 001k catalyzed but escaped, by being unregistered). The structural weaknesses a future action could target: (1) the downstream off-ramps, since more than $1.5 billion of traceable onward flow lands at four identifiable exchanges (WhiteBIT, Binance, OKX, Kraken) that can screen and freeze; (2) the receiving-wallet clusters, which are the seizure/designation surface; and (3) the operator-of-record shells, which are swappable but still create paper trails (Lithuania, Panama). The countervailing resilience: a Telegram-first, Cloudflare-fronted stack and a pseudonymous Panama wrapper make the entity itself hard to reach without multilateral action.
