SECTION 1 - EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

  1. The US sanctioned the whole A7 Network on 1 October. It also proposed cutting six of the network's payment agents off from US banks. The volume figures are large but their method is not disclosed. Confidence: CONFIRMED for the actions.
  2. ShinyHunters claims it breached the FBI. It says it took data on nearly all FBI agents and job applicants through a flaw in Oracle PeopleSoft. The FBI says it is investigating and does not yet know where the breach happened. The same group also defaced the Clop leak site on 25 September. Confidence: CREDIBLE REPORTING for the claim. The scope is unconfirmed.
  3. Leak-site claims fell by about a quarter. September had 808 claims against 1,089 in August. The Gentlemen are now first and Qilin second. Confidence: CONFIRMED on our own collection.
  4. European and US police took over the KillSec leak site on 30 September. Three people were arrested. Confidence: CONFIRMED that it happened. Some detail differs between sources.
  5. No sign that Russia or Belarus acted against ransomware actors. This fits the exclusion norm, but our search did not reach Russian government sources. Confidence: ANALYST INFERENCE.

SECTION 3 - THREAT ACTOR LANDSCAPE

Ransomware brands

  • The Gentlemen took first place with 98 claims.
  • Qilin fell to 73 claims after a record August. Around 1 September it published material it says came from the ATF; the ATF says it cannot confirm that material. The drop could be a pause, affiliates moving to other brands, or collection lag. Nothing separates these. Several firms call Qilin Russian-speaking.
  • Mid-size brands gained while the leaders slipped. Affiliates are probably spreading across brands rather than losing capacity. Confidence: ANALYST INFERENCE.
  • LockBit listed US Bancorp in August. The bank says the cause was a third party. No sign the data was published.
  • KillSec posted once in our data. A tracker shows a similar "Killsec3" label with many posts after the arrests. Whether it is the same group, a successor or an imitator is unresolved.

ShinyHunters

ShinyHunters is a data-theft and extortion group. No source reviewed links it to Russia or the CIS.

  • The FBI claim. On 22 to 23 September it said it holds 2 to 3 TB on almost all FBI agents and applicants. It said the action was not about money and demanded the FBI retract a May advisory about the group. The FBI confirms it is investigating and says the breach point is undetermined.
  • How. Reporting points to Oracle PeopleSoft flaw CVE-2026-35273, which Oracle patched in June. The group found a one-character trick that got past the firewall rule many organizations relied on instead of patching.
  • Clop. On 25 September the group defaced the Clop leak site and claimed it stole Clop's source code and Tor keys. Clop denies any contact and says the server held nothing of value. The key theft is unverified.
  • Arrest. Dutch police arrested a 24-year-old suspect on 15 September. The FBI announced it around 29 to 30 September. His role in the group is unproven.
  • Listings. Seven claims in September, including Fresenius Medical Care, Kimberly-Clark and the Florida DMV, down from 20 in August. Earlier, McKesson was hit in August.

Assessment: demanding a retraction instead of money breaks the group's usual pattern. It may be retaliation for the May advisory and the Dutch arrest, or an attempt to shape law enforcement messaging. Nothing here separates the two. Confidence: CREDIBLE REPORTING for the claims, ANALYST INFERENCE for the assessment.

SECTION 4 - INITIAL ACCESS AND TTP EVOLUTION

  • AI-agent intrusion. Unit 42 described an intrusion where several AI agents worked in parallel and reached full compromise of a victim in ten hours. No ransomware family or actor was named, so no Russia or CIS capability can be inferred.
  • Unpatched web-facing systems. ShinyHunters used the PeopleSoft flaw, and SonicWall disclosed a new zero-day pair on 1 September with no actor named. A news briefing linked it to INC Ransomware; SonicWall's own statement contradicts that, so we treat the link as unconfirmed.
  • Social engineering remains the leading entry route in recent vendor reports, including fake IT support calls over Microsoft Teams. These reports pre-date the window and are context only.

Confidence: CREDIBLE REPORTING.

SECTION 5 - MALWARE AND STEALER ECOSYSTEM

  • 24 September, Darktrace. A ClickFix campaign delivers several stealers (including Lumma, Rhadamanthys and Vidar) and uses the Polygon blockchain to hold its command-and-control details. No actor named, no ransomware link stated.
  • 21 September, Blackpoint. The ChainScript remote access trojan uses the same blockchain trick and fake Spotify, Zoom and Teams installers. No actor named.
  • 28 September, Lunex. A malware-as-a-service platform with 28 control panels in 13 countries spreads a stealer aimed at Ukrainian speakers. No actor named.
  • Law enforcement. No in-window takedown was found. The Endgame action against Amadey, StealC and SocGholish was announced at the end of June.
  • Initial access brokers: nothing found in the window.
  • Bulletproof hosting: nothing found in the window.

The two null results reflect a limited English search, not proof of no activity. Confidence: CREDIBLE REPORTING for the three findings.

SECTION 6 - FINANCIAL AND INFRASTRUCTURE SIGNALS

6.1 The A7 Network action of 1 October

  • OFAC designated the whole A7 Network as a significant transnational criminal organization. Treasury names Ilan Shor as its leader.
  • FinCEN proposed a rule that would bar US financial institutions from handling funds for six named A7 Sub-Agents. The rule is not yet in force.
  • FinCEN also issued an alert listing red flags for banks, such as mis-invoicing and certain IP ranges and VPN domains.
  • This extends the August 2025 action, which hit only the token issuer and one company.

6.2 The numbers that matter

  • FinCEN says at least $179.1 billion in A7A5 transactions passed through the network from February 2025 to June 2026. The method is not disclosed.
  • FinCEN says Sub-Agents handled over $17 billion in US dollars. This is fiat, not token volume, and several outlets headline only this figure.
  • EDP's own measurement found $126.5 billion in gross Tron transfers. The gap to FinCEN's figure cannot be explained from the published text, so cite FinCEN's number as "per FinCEN, method undisclosed."

6.3 Link to ransomware

Treasury and FinCEN say ransomware actors used the network. They name no group, address, case or amount. Treasury frames the action mainly as Iran-related. Confidence: CONFIRMED that the agencies said it. The size of the link is "No reliable data available for this metric."

6.4 Other crypto sanctions in the window

  • 9 September: Xinbi Guarantee, a Chinese-language escrow marketplace.
  • 17 September: BitBank, a Tehran exchange used to move Bitcoin to the IRGC.
  • 30 September: a Tren de Aragua network, including a malware engineer.

None has a Russia or CIS link. Confidence: CONFIRMED.

6.5 Exchanges and hosting

No Garantex, Grinex or Kyrgyz bank development was found, and no bulletproof hosting action. That is a search gap, not proof of none.

SECTION 9 - SUPPLY CHAIN AND THIRD-PARTY COMPROMISE

  • BigCommerce, 17 September. Credentials for a third-party app (Ribon) were stolen and used to inject malicious scripts into a few merchant stores. Customer names, emails, phone numbers and addresses were exposed. No actor named.
  • CrowdSec, 21 September. Source code from about 300 repositories was stolen through the May TanStack supply chain attack by the TeamPCP group. No customer data was taken.
  • GitHub Actions, 16 September. Reporting says two previously compromised actions came back online running malware. Details were not retrieved. Confidence: UNCONFIRMED.
  • TeamPCP. Two men were arrested in Australia in late August, before the window.

No item was tied to a ransomware group. The only extortion actor with a supply-chain-type access path this month is ShinyHunters, in Sections 3 and 8. Confidence: CREDIBLE REPORTING.

SECTION 11 - STRATEGIC LEVERAGE ASSESSMENT

Financial Pressure

Pressure moved one layer down. In 2025 it hit the token issuer. On 1 October it reached the Sub-Agent accounts that turn the token into cash. The test is whether Sub-Agent dollar volume, over $17 billion on FinCEN's figures, falls once a rule takes effect. The rule is only proposed and Treasury frames it as Iran-related. Confidence: CONFIRMED for the actions, ANALYST INFERENCE for the effect.

Infrastructure Pressure

KillSwitch seized one leak site and five servers in one day across four countries. The test is whether KillSec postings continue. The case is Western-based and does not bear on Russian-hosted infrastructure, and no bulletproof hosting action was found. Confidence: CONFIRMED for the seizure, ANALYST INFERENCE for the effect.

Jurisdictional Pressure

Russian nationals are reaching US custody through third countries while no Russian or Belarusian ransomware prosecution surfaced. Kyrgyz press says Kyrgyzstan is central to A7, which makes Kyrgyz bank and registry data the pressure point on that side. The Dutch arrest in the ShinyHunters case does not bear on the CIS question. Confidence: CREDIBLE REPORTING for the extraditions, ANALYST INFERENCE for the Kyrgyz point.

SECTION 13 - ANALYTIC CAVEATS AND COLLECTION GAPS

  • What we could not see. The CISA catalog, Eurojust and parts of Europol were not reachable. DOJ, Treasury and EU lists were only partly covered. Google, Mandiant and FBI primary statements were not obtained. Every "not found" line in this report is an absence of evidence, not proof of absence.
  • Single-source and second-hand items. Several findings rest on trade press relaying a vendor or agency statement. Each is labelled CREDIBLE REPORTING or UNCONFIRMED where that applies.
  • Figures were read through automated summaries of web pages, not checked against raw documents. The key A7 figures agree across FinCEN, Treasury and TRM Labs.
  • Unresolved conflicts. Treasury and FinCEN cite one legal authority and a vendor briefing cites another for the A7 designation. Dates and country counts for KillSwitch differ across sources.
  • Language coverage. Russian 18 queries, Ukrainian 4. Persian, Korean and Chinese were not searched. No Ukrainian result fell inside the window.

ShinyHunters: FBI breach claim and September activity

ShinyHunters (tracked by Google as UNC6240) is a data-theft and extortion group; no source in the reporting reviewed reports a Russia or CIS link. It was the most consequential non-ransomware-brand actor of the window.

  • The FBI claim. On 22 to 23 September the group posted that it had stolen data on "almost ALL FBI Agents and individuals who filed an application," naming FBIJOBS, PEGA, Medlink, HR, CJ and PHIRE services, including Medlink medical and prescription data, at roughly 2 to 3 TB. Claimed fields include names, home addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, badge numbers, duty assignments and spouse and family details. The group said the action was "not financially motivated," that it would "never publish this data," and demanded the FBI retract a May 2026 public service announcement that accused it of harassment and exaggerated claims of access. The retraction deadline is reported inconsistently (Sunday 27 September, Monday 28 September, 30 September) and passed unmet. fbijobs.gov was reported defaced and offline as of 28 September. The group says its own site outage on 1 October was DDoS and infrastructure work unrelated to the investigation. CREDIBLE REPORTING (The Hacker News, CyberScoop, SAN, Vectra; COMMERCIAL and relayed claims).
  • FBI position. The FBI said it is "aware of claims regarding unauthorized activity affecting FBIjobs.gov and is currently investigating," and that "the point of breach is still undetermined," whether third-party or the FBI enterprise (The Hacker News); later it said it is "actively and aggressively investigating" and has not confirmed scope or attributed the incident (CyberScoop). Vectra reports 404 Media checked sample data against public records. Secondary headlines cite 5,000 employees and one cites 60,000 medical files; neither figure was verified. The agent-safety risk is the central concern: an analyst quoted by CyberScoop calls the data "a roadmap" to locate FBI personnel and families. CONFIRMED that the FBI is investigating; scope and breach point unconfirmed.
  • Access method. Per Vectra and Cybernews, the group exploited Oracle PeopleSoft CVE-2026-35273, which Mandiant first reported as exploited in June 2026 against educational institutions. Vectra reports access to apply.fbijobs.gov and then additional FBI-managed storage on Amazon's government cloud. Whether the FBI had patched or relied on the firewall rule is unclear. CREDIBLE REPORTING; see Section 8.

DETAIL 11 - STRATEGIC LEVERAGE ASSESSMENT

Financial Pressure

Pressure moved one layer down the stack. In August 2025 it fell on the issuer and entity layer (Old Vector, A7 LLC); on 1 October it reaches the Sub-Agent accounts that connect the token to fiat. The NPRM would bind 347,926 covered US institutions, 332,068 of them money services businesses. Measurable indicator: Sub-Agent USD throughput, which FinCEN puts at over $17 billion for January 2025 to June 2026, is the baseline against which the final rule can be tested. Limit: the rule is proposed, not effective, and Treasury frames the action as Iran-led. Confidence: CONFIRMED for the instruments, ANALYST INFERENCE for the effect.

Infrastructure Pressure

Operation KillSwitch seized one leak site and five servers in one day across four countries. The measurable test is persistence: whether KillSec-labelled postings continue after 1 October. The case is Western-based and data-extortion focused, so it does not bear on the Russian-hosted infrastructure question. No bulletproof hosting action was found. Confidence: CONFIRMED for the seizure, ANALYST INFERENCE for the effect.

Jurisdictional Pressure

Custody of Russian nationals continues through third countries (Cyprus, Georgia, Thailand, three extraditions since June) while no domestic Russian or Belarusian ransomware prosecution surfaced. The Dutch arrest of a 24-year-old in the ShinyHunters case (15 Sep) is a Western-jurisdiction action against an English-speaking data-extortion network and does not bear on the CIS exclusion question. Kyrgyz regional reporting places Kyrgyzstan at the centre of A7 operations, which makes Kyrgyz banking and registry data the jurisdictional pressure point on the A7 side. Measurable indicator: count of Russian-national extraditions per quarter. Confidence: CREDIBLE REPORTING (independent, exile outlet) for the extraditions, ANALYST INFERENCE for the Kyrgyz point.
